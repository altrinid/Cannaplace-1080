Is CBD legal in Austria?

CBD is legal in Austria — but not every product type is treated the same. Here’s what applies to oils, cosmetics and flowers, and what has changed since 2025.

As of October 2026. This article gives a general overview and does not replace legal advice. Laws and their interpretation can change.

The key points

  • CBD itself is not prohibited in Austria.
  • Hemp products may contain no more than 0.3% THC.
  • CBD oils are sold as aroma oils, because CBD would need novel food authorisation to be sold as food.
  • CBD cosmetics are permitted if they comply with the EU Cosmetics Regulation.
  • Since 2025, smokable hemp flowers fall under the tobacco monopoly: sold in tobacconists (Trafiken) and — with a transitional licence until the end of 2028 — in specialist hemp stores. Shipping is not permitted.
  • As with tobacco products, the minimum age for hemp flowers is 18.

The 0.3% THC limit

THC content decides whether a hemp product is legal. In Austria, hemp and hemp products with no more than 0.3% THC are not considered narcotic. Above that, a product is illegal — regardless of how much CBD it contains. That’s why a current lab report matters: it documents the THC content of each batch. Our article How to read a certificate of analysis explains how to read one.

CBD oils: why the label says “aroma oil”

In the EU, CBD extracts are considered novel foods. Before they can be sold as food or food supplements they need authorisation — which has not been granted so far. In Austria, CBD oils are therefore sold as aroma oils and labelled “not intended for consumption”.

Just as important: CBD products may not be advertised with health claims. Statements such as “helps against …” are reserved for medicinal products, so reputable shops deliberately avoid them. You’ll find our oils under CBD oils.

CBD cosmetics

Cannabidiol is listed in the EU database of cosmetic ingredients (CosIng). CBD cosmetics such as balms or creams may be sold if, like any cosmetic product, they comply with the EU Cosmetics Regulation: with a safety assessment, correct labelling and notification in the EU Cosmetic Products Notification Portal (CPNP). CBD flowers: tobacco monopoly since 2025

The biggest change concerns dried hemp flowers. In January 2025, Austria’s Supreme Administrative Court (VwGH) ruled that smokable hemp flowers with no more than 0.3% THC fall under the Tobacco Monopoly Act and are subject to tobacco tax, at a rate of 34%. As a result, they may in principle only be sold in tobacconists.

The Tax Amendment Act 2025 (Abgabenänderungsgesetz 2025), passed by the National Council in December 2025, explicitly added hemp flowers for smoking to the tobacco monopoly. At the same time it created a transition rule: specialist hemp stores that were already trading predominantly in hemp flowers on 10 January 2025 can apply to the Monopolverwaltung GmbH for a licence and continue selling flowers until the end of 2028. From 2029, hemp flowers are to be available in tobacconists only.

For customers this mainly means: mail order of hemp flowers is not permitted. Reputable shops don’t ship flowers — and neither do we. Our CBD flowers are available exclusively in our store at Josefstädter Straße 56.

What applies when driving?

In Austria, anyone impaired by narcotic substances may not drive a vehicle. CBD itself is not intoxicating, but many products contain traces of THC. If you use CBD products regularly, keep this in mind for traffic checks and drug tests.

Minimum age

Hemp flowers are subject to the same age limits as tobacco products: sale from 18 only. We sell our entire range to adults only.

Frequently asked questions

Is CBD legal in Austria?

Yes. CBD products with no more than 0.3% THC are legal. Different rules apply depending on the product type, though — such as novel food law for oils and the tobacco monopoly for flowers.

Can I order CBD flowers online in Austria?

No. Hemp flowers fall under the tobacco monopoly and mail order is prohibited. They are available in tobacconists and, until the end of 2028, in licensed specialist hemp stores.

Why isn’t CBD oil sold as a food supplement?

Because CBD extracts count as novel foods in the EU and have not yet been authorised as food. CBD oils are therefore sold as aroma oils.

Can I drive after using CBD?

CBD is not intoxicating, but many products contain traces of THC. Anyone impaired by narcotic substances may not drive — so be careful and, if in doubt, ask a doctor.

CBD in a nutshell

CBD stands for cannabidiol. It is one of more than a hundred cannabinoids found in the hemp plant (Cannabis sativa L.). The best-known cannabinoid is THC (tetrahydrocannabinol) — the substance responsible for the cannabis high. CBD, on the other hand, is not intoxicating. In its report on cannabidiol, the World Health Organization (WHO) noted that in humans CBD shows no effects indicative of any abuse or dependence potential.

CBD products are made from industrial hemp: varieties listed in the EU plant variety catalogue that produce only very little THC. In Austria, hemp products may contain no more than 0.3% THC — more on that in our article Is CBD legal in Austria?

How do CBD and THC differ?

CBD and THC are chemically closely related but differ in one crucial point: THC is psychoactive, CBD is not. The key differences at a glance:

Intoxicatingnoyes

Legal framework in Austriapermitted if the product contains no more than 0.3% THCcovered by the Narcotic Substances Act (except traces in hemp below 0.3%)

Both substances interact with the body’s endocannabinoid system — a network of receptors and the body’s own messenger substances involved in many processes. Exactly how CBD works there is the subject of ongoing research. That’s why reputable sellers make no health claims: CBD products are not medicinal products.

Which CBD products are there?

  • CBD oils: CBD extract dissolved in a carrier oil — usually hemp seed oil. In Austria they are sold as aroma oils. See our selection of CBD oils.
  • CBD flowers: dried flowers of industrial hemp with high CBD and low THC content. Since 2025 they fall under the tobacco monopoly, so our CBD flowers are available in store only.
  • CBD cosmetics: balms, creams and lip care using CBD as a caring ingredient — see CBD cosmetics.
  • Other forms: such as resins or e-liquids. Foods containing CBD are currently not authorised as novel foods in the EU.

Full spectrum, broad spectrum, isolate — what does it mean?

You’ll come across these three terms again and again with oils and extracts. They describe which of the hemp plant’s compounds a product contains:

  • Full spectrum: contains other cannabinoids such as CBG, terpenes and flavonoids in addition to CBD — and THC in traces below the limit.
  • Broad spectrum: like full spectrum, but with the THC largely removed.
  • Isolate: almost pure CBD (usually over 99%) without other plant compounds.

Which one you choose is mainly a matter of personal preference. Many people like full-spectrum products because they reflect the plant’s natural profile. If you want to avoid traces of THC as far as possible, broad spectrum or isolate is the better choice. Explained in detail: Full spectrum, broad spectrum or isolate?

What do the percentages mean?

The percentage tells you how much CBD a product contains. For oils, it refers to the contents of the bottle: 10% CBD in 10 ml of oil equals roughly 1,000 mg CBD. So look at the bottle size as well as the percentage.

How do you recognise good quality?

  • Certificate of analysis (COA): issued by an independent lab, ideally accredited to ISO/IEC 17025.
  • Matching batch number: the number on the certificate matches the one on the product.
  • THC below 0.3%: total THC is below the legal limit.
  • Contaminant testing: pesticides, heavy metals and residual solvents were tested — and the limits met.
  • Transparent information: concentration in percent and milligrams, carrier oil and type of extract are clearly stated.
  • Trustworthy advice: anyone promising cures isn’t selling responsibly.

Important notes

  • CBD products are not medicinal products and do not replace medical treatment.
  • If you take medication, are pregnant or breastfeeding, talk to your doctor before use — CBD can interact with certain medicines.
  • Even legal products can contain traces of THC. This can matter for drug tests and when driving.
  • We only sell to persons aged 18 and over.

Frequently asked questions

Does CBD get you high?

No. CBD is not intoxicating. The cannabis high comes from THC — and legal CBD products in Austria contain less than 0.3% THC.

Is CBD the same as cannabis?

Not quite. CBD is one compound of the cannabis plant. Colloquially, “cannabis” usually means THC-rich strains, whereas CBD products are made from industrial hemp with very little THC.

Can CBD show up in a drug test?

Drug tests usually look for THC or its metabolites, not CBD. As legal CBD products can contain traces of THC, a positive result cannot be completely ruled out.
